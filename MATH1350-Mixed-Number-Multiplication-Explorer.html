What counts as one whole?

A 1-by-1 square represents one whole square unit. Divide each side into 10 equal lengths: the square now has 10 rows and 10 columns.

Teaching notes
  1. Whole number × tenths: begin with 2 × 3/10. See two groups of three tenth-strips.
  2. Tenths × tenths: use 3/10 × 4/10. A tenth of a tenth is a hundredth; count the small squares.
  3. Mixed number × mixed number: split each side into its whole part and fractional part. The distributive property gives up to four regions.
  4. Collect the pieces. Exchange 10 hundredths for 1 tenth, then 10 tenths for 1 whole. Explain why these exchanges preserve area.

Factors range from 0 to 4 9/10 in the final stage. Zero parts are allowed to explore boundary cases. Fractions stay in base-ten form rather than being reduced.
